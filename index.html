How the budget builder plans your setup

The builder works at the category level. It doesn't tell you to buy one particular microphone. It tells you what kind of microphone, light and camera gives you the most improvement for your budget. It then gives a rough price range for that kind of gear so you can plan. Prices change every week, so the ranges are approximate and only meant for planning. Always check current prices at a few retailers before you buy.

It starts from the cheapest setup that works: a USB dynamic mic, a lamp or window light, and the webcam or phone camera you already own. Then it spends the rest of your budget in upgrade steps. Your priority category climbs first, using up to about two-thirds of the budget, and then gets two upgrade chances per round to the others' one. In an ordinary room, a $300 audio-first plan therefore ends up with an XLR mic and interface, while a $300 balanced plan spreads the money across a better USB mic, a key light and a 1080p webcam.

Why audio usually comes first

People will sit through a soft image, but harsh, echoey or noisy audio makes them leave. A dynamic mic placed a few centimetres from your mouth picks up much less room sound than a condenser on the far side of the desk. Shure's own explainer gives that as the main practical difference. That's why the builder suggests dynamic mics unless you tell it your room is quiet and treated. If you're stuck between USB and XLR, read our USB vs XLR guide.

Light before lens

Webcam sensors are small, and in dim light they get grainy and smeary. One soft key light at about 45° in front of you often does more for image quality than a more expensive webcam. A fill light and a backlight come next. Our small-room lighting guide shows the placement. A mirrorless camera only appears in the plan once the budget covers the camera and the extras it needs, such as a capture card and a dummy battery. Our webcam vs mirrorless guide explains the trade-off.

Room type matters

Room type changes the plan:

  • Echoey room: adds soft furnishings, such as a rug, heavy curtains or blankets, before a more sensitive mic.
  • Noisy room: keeps you on a close dynamic mic and points you to OBS's free noise suppression filter, covered in our background noise guide.
  • Dark room: moves a real key light up the list.

Frequently asked questions

What should I buy first for streaming?

For most people, a microphone. Viewers forgive a soft webcam image much more readily than muddy or echoey audio. After the mic, a single soft key light usually improves a webcam more than upgrading the webcam itself.

Can I start streaming with a $100 budget?

Yes. Put most of it into a USB dynamic microphone, use a daylight lamp or window light, and use your phone or existing webcam as the camera. Add a light and a dedicated webcam later.

Is a mirrorless camera worth it for streaming?

Only once audio and lighting are sorted, and usually only on budgets around $1,000 or more. A camera also needs a capture card or clean HDMI output and a dummy battery for long streams, and those add to the cost.

Are the prices in this tool exact?

No. They are approximate ranges for typical new gear in each category, meant for planning a budget. Prices vary by retailer, region and sales, so check current prices before you buy.

Why does room type change the recommendation?

An echoey room with bare walls makes sensitive condenser microphones sound distant, so the tool steers you to a dynamic mic and soft furnishings. A dark room needs a key light before anything else, and a noisy shared space benefits most from a close-placed dynamic mic and noise suppression.
